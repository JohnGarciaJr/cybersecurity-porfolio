# Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery (BC/DR) Overview

## 1. Purpose
This document provides a high‑level overview of the organization’s approach to Business Continuity (BC) and Disaster Recovery (DR). It outlines how the organization prepares for, responds to, and recovers from disruptive events to ensure the continued availability of critical services and the protection of essential data.

---

## 2. Scope
This overview applies to:
- All business units and departments
- All critical business functions identified in the Business Impact Analysis (BIA)
- All systems, applications, and data supporting essential operations
- All employees, contractors, and third‑party service providers involved in continuity or recovery activities

---

## 3. Key Concepts

### 3.1 Business Continuity (BC)
Business Continuity focuses on maintaining essential business operations during and after a disruption. It ensures the organization can continue delivering critical services even under adverse conditions.

### 3.2 Disaster Recovery (DR)
Disaster Recovery focuses on restoring IT systems, applications, and data after a major outage or disaster. It supports the broader Business Continuity strategy.

### 3.3 Recovery Time Objective (RTO)
The maximum acceptable time a business function or system can be unavailable before causing significant impact.

### 3.4 Recovery Point Objective (RPO)
The maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time.

These values are defined in the Business Impact Analysis (BIA).

---

## 4. Governance Alignment
The BC/DR program is supported by the following governance documents:

- **Information Security Policy:** Establishes the organization’s security expectations and responsibilities.
- **Acceptable Use Policy (AUP):** Defines appropriate use of systems and data, reducing the likelihood of incidents.
- **Data Classification Standard:** Ensures data is protected according to sensitivity, informing recovery priorities.
- **Security Awareness One‑Pager:** Promotes employee readiness and reduces human‑related disruptions.

Together, these documents create a foundation for resilience and preparedness.

---

## 5. Roles & Responsibilities

### Executive Leadership
- Approves BC/DR strategies and major recovery decisions  
- Ensures adequate resources are available  

### Business Unit Leaders
- Identify critical functions and dependencies  
- Participate in BIA and continuity planning  

### IT & Security Teams
- Maintain backup systems and recovery procedures  
- Restore systems and data during DR events  
- Monitor systems for disruptions  

### All Employees
- Follow governance policies  
- Report incidents promptly  
- Participate in training and exercises  

---

## 6. High‑Level BC/DR Process

### 6.1 Identify
- Detect disruptions or potential threats  
- Notify appropriate teams  

### 6.2 Assess
- Determine severity and impact  
- Reference BIA for criticality and recovery objectives  

### 6.3 Respond
- Activate continuity procedures  
- Communicate with stakeholders  

### 6.4 Recover
- Restore systems, applications, and data  
- Meet RTO and RPO requirements  

### 6.5 Restore Normal Operations
- Resume standard business processes  
- Validate system integrity  

### 6.6 Review & Improve
- Conduct post‑incident review  
- Update BC/DR plans and governance documents  

---

## 7. Dependencies
BC/DR relies on:
- Accurate BIA results  
- Reliable backups and recovery tools  
- Vendor and third‑party support  
- Clear communication channels  
- Employee awareness and training  

---

## 8. Continuous Improvement
The BC/DR program is reviewed annually or after significant changes in:
- Business operations  
- Technology infrastructure  
- Regulatory requirements  
- Risk landscape  

Updates are incorporated into governance documents and communicated to relevant stakeholders.
