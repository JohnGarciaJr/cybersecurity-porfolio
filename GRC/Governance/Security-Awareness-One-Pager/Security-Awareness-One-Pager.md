# Security Awareness One‑Pager

## Purpose
This one‑pager provides employees with simple, practical guidance to help protect company data, systems, and accounts. Following these best practices reduces the risk of security incidents and supports a safe working environment for everyone.

---

## 1. Recognize Phishing Attempts
Phishing is the most common way attackers gain access to accounts and systems.

**Watch for:**
- Unexpected emails asking you to click a link or open an attachment  
- Messages that create urgency (“Your account will be closed!”)  
- Misspelled email addresses or unusual sender names  
- Requests for passwords, MFA codes, or personal information  

**If something feels off:**  
Do not click anything. Report it to the security team immediately.

---

## 2. Use Strong, Unique Passwords
Weak or reused passwords are a major security risk.

**Best practices:**
- Use at least 12 characters  
- Include a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols  
- Never reuse passwords across accounts  
- Use a company‑approved password manager  

**Never share your password with anyone.**

---

## 3. Enable Multi‑Factor Authentication (MFA)
MFA adds an extra layer of protection by requiring something you know (password) and something you have (phone/app).

**Always enable MFA** on:
- Email  
- VPN  
- Internal systems  
- Any application that supports it  

---

## 4. Protect Company Devices
Whether working in the office or remotely, secure your devices at all times.

**Do:**
- Lock your screen when stepping away  
- Keep devices updated  
- Use company‑approved software only  
- Store sensitive data in approved locations  

**Do not:**
- Install unauthorized apps  
- Use personal USB drives  
- Leave devices unattended in public places  

---

## 5. Handle Data Responsibly
Follow the Data Classification Standard when storing or sharing information.

**General rules:**
- Encrypt sensitive data when sending or storing  
- Share confidential or restricted data only with authorized personnel  
- Avoid sending sensitive information through personal email or messaging apps  
- Dispose of sensitive documents securely  

---

## 6. Stay Alert on Public Wi‑Fi
Public networks are risky.

**If you must use public Wi‑Fi:**
- Connect through the company VPN  
- Avoid accessing sensitive systems  
- Never transmit confidential or restricted data  

---

## 7. Report Security Incidents Immediately
If you notice something suspicious — even if you’re unsure — report it right away.

Examples include:
- Strange pop‑ups  
- Unexpected password resets  
- Lost or stolen devices  
- Emails that seem suspicious  

Early reporting helps prevent larger incidents.

---

## Quick Checklist
- ☐ Think before you click  
- ☐ Use strong, unique passwords  
- ☐ Enable MFA everywhere  
- ☐ Lock your screen when away  
- ☐ Follow data handling rules  
- ☐ Use VPN on public Wi‑Fi  
- ☐ Report anything suspicious
