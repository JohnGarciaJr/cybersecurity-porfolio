# Responsible AI Policy  
## Facial Recognition Access Control System  
### AI Governance Case Study

This Responsible AI Policy establishes the principles, requirements, and governance expectations for the development, deployment, and operation of the Facial Recognition Access Control System. It ensures the system is used ethically, safely, and in compliance with organizational and regulatory standards.

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to:

- Promote responsible and ethical use of biometric AI systems  
- Ensure fairness, transparency, and accountability  
- Protect individual privacy and biometric data  
- Reduce risks associated with automated decision-making  
- Align system operations with organizational values and legal requirements  

This policy applies to all teams involved in the design, deployment, monitoring, and oversight of the system.

---

## 2. Guiding Principles

### **2.1 Fairness**
The system must be evaluated for demographic performance disparities.  
Bias mitigation steps must be documented and reviewed regularly.

### **2.2 Transparency**
Users must be informed about:

- What data is collected  
- How it is used  
- How long it is retained  
- Their rights and available recourse  

Transparency notices must be accessible and written in clear language.

### **2.3 Privacy Protection**
Biometric data must be:

- Minimized  
- Encrypted  
- Access‑controlled  
- Retained only as long as necessary  

Privacy impact assessments (DPIAs) are required before deployment.

### **2.4 Accountability**
Human oversight is required for all high‑risk decisions.  
Teams must maintain documentation demonstrating compliance with this policy.

### **2.5 Security**
The system must incorporate strong technical safeguards, including:

- Liveness detection  
- Encryption  
- Access controls  
- Audit logging  
- Continuous monitoring  

### **2.6 Human-Centric Design**
Users must have:

- A clear escalation path  
- A manual override option  
- The ability to challenge or appeal decisions  

---

## 3. Roles & Responsibilities

### **3.1 AI/ML Team**
- Conduct bias testing and model evaluations  
- Maintain model documentation and versioning  
- Monitor performance and drift  

### **3.2 Security Engineering**
- Implement technical safeguards  
- Protect biometric data  
- Maintain audit logs and incident response procedures  

### **3.3 IAM Governance**
- Ensure access control policies are followed  
- Review privileged access  
- Validate identity workflows  

### **3.4 Privacy Office**
- Conduct DPIAs  
- Ensure compliance with biometric privacy laws  
- Approve data retention schedules  

### **3.5 Operations & Facilities**
- Manage manual access procedures  
- Train staff on system use and escalation  

---

## 4. Data Governance Requirements

### **4.1 Data Minimization**
Only necessary biometric data may be collected.  
Raw images must not be stored unless explicitly required.

### **4.2 Retention & Deletion**
Biometric templates must be deleted:

- When no longer needed  
- Upon user departure  
- Upon user request (where legally applicable)  

### **4.3 Access Restrictions**
Access to biometric data is limited to authorized personnel with a legitimate business need.

---

## 5. Model Governance Requirements

### **5.1 Bias & Performance Testing**
- Conducted prior to deployment  
- Repeated at least annually  
- Documented in the Model Card  

### **5.2 Monitoring & Drift Detection**
- Continuous monitoring of accuracy and error rates  
- Alerts for performance degradation  
- Retraining scheduled as needed  

### **5.3 Change Management**
- All model updates require governance review  
- Major changes require re‑evaluation of risks and controls  

---

## 6. Human Oversight Requirements

### **6.1 Manual Override**
Security staff must be able to manually approve or deny access.

### **6.2 Appeals Process**
Users must have a clear path to challenge incorrect decisions.

### **6.3 Training**
Staff must be trained on:

- System limitations  
- Bias risks  
- Privacy obligations  
- Escalation procedures  

---

## 7. Compliance Requirements

The system must comply with:

- Biometric privacy laws (e.g., BIPA, GDPR)  
- Organizational security policies  
- IAM governance standards  
- NIST AI RMF  
- ISO/IEC 27001  
- NIST 800‑53  

Documentation must be maintained for audit readiness.

---

## 8. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in:

- System suspension  
- Mandatory retraining  
- Access revocation  
- Disciplinary action (as applicable)  

---

## 9. Review Cycle

This policy must be reviewed:

- Annually  
- After major system updates  
- After significant incidents or audit findings  

---

## 10. Summary

This Responsible AI Policy ensures the Facial Recognition Access Control System is deployed and operated in a manner that is fair, transparent, secure, and aligned with organizational and regulatory expectations. It provides the foundation for ethical and accountable AI governance.
